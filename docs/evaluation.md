# Guard evaluation

## Headline (ADR-005)

How much sooner the guard ends a failing run than a stopwatch would, both
held to the same 5% false-stop budget. The stopwatch stops any run that
outlasts the good calibration runs. Every failure in corpus A runs to the
280-step cap, so the stopwatch alone catches all of them. The guard is a
signal OR that stopwatch, both triggers set from good runs only
(`backstop.guard.stopwatch`). Spatial held-out half, 20 random splits,
paired: guard and stopwatch on the same split.

| guard | saved over stopwatch, mean | 10th–90th percentile | splits with no gain | false-stop |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **disagreement OR stopwatch (headline)** | **13.4 steps (0.67 s)** | −1.4 to 25.9 | 3/20 | 0.043 |
| temporal consistency OR stopwatch | 14.5 steps (0.73 s) | 2.5 to 28.8 | 2/20 | 0.044 |
| trust head (cross-fit) OR stopwatch | 9.3 steps (0.47 s) | −1.0 to 20.5 | 3/20 | 0.042 |

The stopwatch alone stops failures at step 135 on average (6.7 s). The
signal fires before it on about half the failures. On the other half
nothing in the signals separates the failure from a good run before the
clock runs out ("Is the failure visible at the onset?", below).
Disagreement is the headline guard because it is the simplest: one signal,
nothing learned. Temporal consistency is within a step of it.

The stopwatch is strong here because every failure in this corpus is a
stall that runs to the cap. Where failures end early, a clock catches few
of them and the signals carry more of the result.

Reproduce with `PYTHONPATH=src python scripts/evaluate_stopwatch.py`.
The headline is written to `artifacts/guard/headline/guard_eval.json`.

## Persistence stop

A stop now requires the score to stay at or above its line for several
consecutive steps. The line and the hold are chosen on the spatial
calibration half only, inside the same 5% false-stop count as the
single-crossing line, and they may give up at most 5 percentage points of
calibration detection. Onset ranges are not an input. Lead time below is
onset minus the stop step on the held-out half, caught failures only, at
20 Hz. Positive would mean the stop landed before that end of the onset
range.

| signal | rule | line | hold | detection | false-stop | median lead, onset start | median lead, onset end |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| action_chunk_disagreement | single crossing | 0.213 | 1 | 32/41 | 4/69 | −44 steps (−2.2 s) | −24 steps (−1.2 s) |
| action_chunk_disagreement | persistence | 0.170 | 2 | 31/41 | 3/69 | −37 steps (−1.9 s) | −17 steps (−0.9 s) |
| trust_head | single crossing | 0.524 | 1 | 34/41 | 3/69 | −61.5 steps (−3.1 s) | −41 steps (−2.1 s) |
| trust_head | persistence | 0.513 | 1 | 35/41 | 3/69 | −63 steps (−3.2 s) | −42 steps (−2.1 s) |

Requiring disagreement to stay up for 2 steps lets the line drop from 0.213
to 0.170. On the held-out half that stops about 0.3 s earlier and still
lands after the onset: of the 31 catches, 6 are before the range, 8 inside
it, and 17 after it. The combined trust score does not get earlier. The
search keeps a hold of 1 and lowers the line from 0.524 to 0.513, which
catches one more failure and moves the median stop from 61.5 steps after
the onset start to 63. Temporal consistency and the
visual check keep a hold of 1. Progress can be made to stop nearer the
onset only by missing more failures (14/41, median −24 steps).

Reproduce with `PYTHONPATH=src python scripts/evaluate_persistence.py`.
The chosen parameters are written to `artifacts/guard/persistence/report.json`.

## Signals from the papers

Two scores were added from the stored action chunks, and the stop line was
also allowed to follow the path of a typical calibration success. The same
5% false-stop rule and the same held-out half were used. Onset was not an
input. Nothing here reads the model's internal state, and nothing was
trained on the onset marks.

| signal | detection | false-stop | median lead, onset start | median lead, onset end |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| action-chunk magnitude | 1/41 | 7/69 | +63 steps, on that one catch | +83 steps |
| speed-adjusted overlap | 34/41 | 2/69 | −36 steps (−1.8 s) | −20 steps (−1.0 s) |
| combined score, above the success path | 36/41 | 2/69 | −62 steps (−3.1 s) | −42 steps (−2.1 s) |

The speed-adjusted overlap is the overlap gap divided by the size of the
planned motion. It catches about as many failures as the combined score and
stops about a second earlier. Of those 34 catches, 7 are before the onset
range, 8 inside it, and 19 after it. The size of the plan almost never
fires. A line that follows a normal success catches 36 failures with the
combined score, but no earlier: 3.1 s late, the same as the single
crossing. When the head still had the CLIP score as an input, the same
line moved the stop from 3.2 s to 2.7 s late. That gain went with the
visual input, so it was not coming from the action stream.

Reproduce with `PYTHONPATH=src python scripts/evaluate_early.py`.

## Per-step model

A logistic regression is fit on the calibration half only, one row per
step. The label is the episode outcome copied onto every step. Each
episode has one vote, split across its steps, and the success votes are
scaled to the same total as the failure votes. At step t the row holds the
value now, the mean so far, and the max so far, for the four original
signals, action-chunk size, speed-adjusted overlap, and the distance of the
saved last-layer vector from calibration successes. Onset ranges are not
an input. The stop line is the persistence rule on those calibration
successes.

| visual input | line | hold | detection | false-stop | median lead, onset start | median lead, onset end |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **none (current)** | 0.762 | 3 | 41/41 | 5/69 | −26 steps (−1.3 s) | −6 steps (−0.3 s) |
| detector (briefly) | 0.787 | 2 | 41/41 | 6/69 | −18 steps (−0.9 s) | +2 steps (+0.1 s) |
| CLIP (originally) | 0.907 | 1 | 41/41 | 6/69 | −34 steps (−1.7 s) | −14 steps (−0.7 s) |

The model has no visual input (`step_model.STEP_SIGNALS`), for the same
reason as the trust head. On the calibration half its line stops 8 of 162
successes, inside the 5% budget, and catches every calibration failure. On
the held-out half it catches all 41 failures: 12 stop before the onset
range, 7 inside it, and 22 after it. It also stops 5 of 69 held-out
successes, which is over 5%, on one split.

The three rows differ by 8 steps at a time, mostly because the line and
hold search lands somewhere else. The headline comparison over 20 splits
swings by more than that from split to split (−11 to +33 steps), so none
of these differences is a measured effect until it is repeated over splits.

The same frozen line on the unseen object suite stops 83 of 83 successes,
so it does not transfer. With the detector input it had stopped only 40,
which was the visual artifact described under the generated table. Reproduce with
`PYTHONPATH=src python scripts/train_step_model.py`.

## Against a stopwatch

Every failure in corpus A runs to the 280-step cap. Successes finish by
about step 130. So a clock is already a guard: stop any run that is still
going at step N. Set from calibration successes at 5%, N is 132 (6.6 s).
That clock catches 41 of 41 held-out failures and stops 6 of 69 held-out
successes. The trust head catches 34 of 41. Counting failures caught,
the guard barely beats a timer. What it can add is an earlier stop.

### Is the failure visible at the onset?

At step t, take each signal's highest value so far. Compare failures with
the successes still running at t. 0.5 is a coin flip, 1.0 is perfect.

| step | successes running | disagreement | temporal consistency | progress |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 20 | 231 | 0.61 | 0.56 | 0.45 |
| 40 | 231 | 0.65 | 0.65 | 0.47 |
| 60 | 231 | 0.68 | 0.72 | 0.45 |
| 80 | 207 | 0.75 | 0.79 | 0.59 |
| 100 | 117 | 0.77 | 0.80 | 0.65 |

The reviewed onset of a no-grasp or failed-grasp episode is around step
40. There the signals sit at 0.65. A 5% line needs the worst successes to
sit below most failures, and at 0.65 they do not. So the late stop is
mostly a limit of these signals, not a badly placed line. The privileged
cup position is just as late (above), which points the same way.

### Does the guard just detect the perturbation?

Disagreement, one episode-level score per run. If the guard only reacted
to a changed scene, perturbed successes would score above clean ones.

| family | successes | perturbed success vs clean success | failure vs success, same family |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| action_noise | 33 | 0.48 | 0.89 |
| camera | 29 | 0.41 | 0.88 |
| distractor | 33 | 0.41 | 0.92 |
| lighting | 35 | 0.45 | 0.92 |
| object_pose | 28 | 0.32 | 0.93 |
| language_paraphrase | 3 | 1.00 | 0.76 |

Perturbed successes look like clean successes. Failures in the same family
score higher. Temporal consistency and progress give the same picture. The
exception is paraphrase: all 3 of its successes score as abnormal, so
rewording alone unsettles the policy. 3 runs is a direction, not a rate.

### Does the 5% budget hold on held-out data?

The same comparison over 20 random 70/30 splits instead of one. Mean over
splits, 10th–90th percentile in brackets.

| rule | held-out false-stop | splits over 5% | detection | mean stop step |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| disagreement, persistence (searched, as above) | 0.066 [0.014–0.104] | 13/20 | 0.706 | — |
| trust head, single crossing (top table) | 0.063 [0.028–0.119] | 10/20 | 0.838 | — |
| trust head, persistence (searched, as above) | 0.068 [0.029–0.117] | 13/20 | 0.865 | — |
| stopwatch only | 0.051 [0.014–0.087] | 10/20 | 1.000 | 135 (6.7 s) |
| disagreement OR stopwatch | 0.043 [0.013–0.072] | 8/20 | 1.000 | 121 (6.1 s) |
| temporal consistency OR stopwatch | 0.044 [0.014–0.072] | 8/20 | 1.000 | 120 (6.0 s) |
| trust head (cross-fit) OR stopwatch | 0.042 [0.014–0.088] | 6/20 | 1.000 | 125 (6.3 s) |

The searched rules drift over budget for two reasons. The line and hold
are picked from a grid right up to the budget edge on the calibration
half, which fits those particular good runs. The trust head also leaks:
it is trained on the same successes that then set its line, so they look
safer than new ones. A single-split number cannot show either effect.
With 69 held-out successes, one run is 1.4 points.

The OR rules (`backstop.guard.stopwatch`) are set from good runs only. The
line is the 4th-highest peak among 162 calibration successes. The clock
stops any run longer than the 4th-longest of them. Each trigger then has
at most a 4/163 chance of stopping a new good run, so the pair stays under
5% on average, with no grid and no failure used. "Cross-fit" means each
calibration run is scored by a head trained without it. The OR rule
catches every failure and stops 14 steps (0.7 s) earlier than the clock
on average. The signal fires first on about half the failures. The other
half are not visible before the clock.

## Visual precondition: a per-object detector gate

The check asks one question before the arm moves: is the instructed object
in the first frame? It uses an open-vocabulary detector (OWLv2,
`google/owlv2-base-patch16-ensemble`) and gives each object its own line
(`backstop.guard.presence`). The line is set only from frames that show
that object: below the k-th lowest of them, the object is called missing.
That bounds how often a frame with the object present is stopped, k/(n+1),
without ever needing a missing-object example. An object with no reference
frames is reported as unverified, never guessed.

One line for every object does not work. A real tomato-sauce can scores
0.01 and a moka-pot lookalike on the spatial table scores 0.3. The CLIP
and SmolVLM2 attempts below failed for the same reason.

References: the black bowl from the 162 spatial calibration successes.
The moka pot from 40 rendered LIBERO-10 scenes that contain one
(`scripts/render_reference_frames.py`), because corpus A never shows one.
Each grocery item from half the object-suite scenes that contain it.

### The missing-object runs

| | result |
|---|---:|
| missing moka pot (`language_absent`) stopped at step 0 | **28/30** |
| spatial held-out good runs stopped (black bowl present) | 2/69 |
| object-suite runs not used as references stopped (instructed item present) | 8/60 |
| same 30 runs, headline guard (disagreement OR stopwatch) | median stop step 86 (4.3 s) |

The gate stops 28 of the 30 missing-object runs before the arm moves. The
headline guard catches them too, but only once the policy starts to waver,
about 4 seconds in. The 2 misses score 0.298 and 0.310 against a line of
0.291: the lookalike on the table. The margin is thin. Two of the 40
rendered moka pots score 0.290 and 0.291, so a real moka pot and the
lookalike overlap at the bottom. The other 38 score 0.33 or more. The 8
object-suite stops come from grocery lines set on other layouts (below).

`language_absent` stays out of the headline (ADR-004): its instruction
names an object that no good run ever names. The gate is reported here on
its own, not added to the headline guard. On corpus A's other runs the
object is always present, so the gate could only add false stops there.
Adding it to a deployed guard means giving it a share of the 5% budget.

### Where the detector works and where it does not

Same object name, frames that show it against frames that do not. "Other
scene" means a scene of the other suite. "Same scene" means an object-suite
scene that lacks the item, beside the ones that have it. Scene contents
come from the LIBERO task files, for scoring only. The `distractor` family
adds a milk carton to spatial scenes, so those count as milk present.

| object | present, other scenes, wrongly flagged | missing, other scene, caught | missing, same scene, caught |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| black bowl | — | 100/100 | — |
| moka pot | — | 388/400 spatial, 100/100 object | — |
| bbq sauce | 4/30 | 400/400 | 40/40 |
| chocolate pudding | 0/30 | 0/400 | 40/40 |
| orange juice | 19/30 | 400/400 | 40/40 |
| ketchup | 18/30 | 400/400 | 30/40 |
| salad dressing | 10/30 | 400/400 | 24/40 |
| milk | 10/30 | 343/350 | 9/40 |
| butter | 0/30 | 320/400 | 0/40 |
| alphabet soup | 19/30 | 0/400 | 2/40 |
| cream cheese | 0/30 | 0/400 | 0/40 |
| tomato sauce | 1/30 | 0/400 | 0/40 |

Three things this shows.

- **The detector tells rooms apart better than objects.** A distinctive
  object missing from a different kind of scene is caught almost every
  time: no black bowl in the grocery scenes, no ketchup on the spatial
  table. Telling one grocery carton from another in the same scene works
  for 3 of 10 items.
- **Some objects it cannot see at all.** Alphabet soup, cream cheese and
  tomato sauce score near zero even when they are in plain view.
- **A line does not carry over to new layouts, even for the same object.**
  The "present, other scenes" column sets each grocery line from 3 scenes
  and checks it on the other 3. Ketchup, orange juice and alphabet soup
  then stop well over half their present frames. The score moves with
  where the object sits and what covers it. The spatial gate works because
  its references cover the same 10 layouts it runs on. The reference
  frames have to come from the setting the guard runs in, the same lesson
  as the stop lines in "Against a stopwatch".

Splitting the grocery references by scene matters. A split by frame puts
near-copies of one layout on both sides, since the 10 runs of a task open
on almost the same scene, and it makes the wrongly-flagged column look
like 0–6 of 30.
